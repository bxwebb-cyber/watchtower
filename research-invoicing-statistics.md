# Small Business Invoicing & Late Payments -- Research Report
**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Depth:** Quick overview
**Sources:** 10+ unique sources (QuickBooks/Intuit, Atradius, Xero, UK Small Business Commissioner, Kaplan Group, BILL, Clockify, Retrievables, Agiled, Allianz Trade)

---

## TL;DR

Late payments have surged to a record high in 2026: 59% of US small businesses carry invoices overdue by 30+ days, averaging $17,700 each. The problem is structural, not seasonal -- driven by federal shutdowns, customer cash-flow pressure, and manual processes. Businesses lose $39,406/year on average to late payments, 39% struggle to cover payroll after a single late payment, and 14,000 UK businesses close annually because of it. Automation is the single strongest predictor of faster payment: businesses using accounting software are 20 percentage points less likely to have overdue invoices than those that don't. This is the market Dunn was built for.

## Key Findings

- **59% of US small businesses** have invoices overdue by 30+ days in 2026, up from 47% in 2025 (QuickBooks/Intuit, survey of ~5,000 owners, July 2026). Source: https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2026/

- **$17,700 average unpaid balance** per business carrying overdue invoices (QuickBooks 2026). Source: same report.

- **49% of owners** say standard payment processing times create critical or moderate cash-flow gaps even after the customer has paid (QuickBooks 2026).

- **59% paid extra fees** last year just to access money they'd already earned -- things like overdraft fees, credit card interest, or factoring (QuickBooks 2026).

- **42% delayed paying their own vendors/contractors** because of late customer payments (QuickBooks 2026). Among businesses already carrying overdue invoices, that rises to 53%.

- **Average wait to get paid** is 28.8 days in the US, up slightly from 2025 (Xero US Small Business Insights, 2026).

- **$39,406 average annual cost** from late payments per company. 10% of companies lose over $100,000/year (Kaplan Group, citing multiple surveys).

- **39% of small businesses** struggled to cover payroll or other bills after just ONE late payment (QuickBooks 2026).

- **Businesses using accounting software** are significantly less likely to have overdue invoices: 56% of businesses with few late payments use accounting software vs 36% of those with many late payments (QuickBooks 2025 data compiled by Clockify).

- **65% of businesses spend 14 hours/week** on collections admin tasks -- that's over 700 hours/year on chasing money you already earned (Kaplan Group).

- **86% of businesses** report up to 30% of monthly invoiced sales are overdue (Kaplan Group / multiple sources).

- **74% of small businesses** are not running a fully automated bill-pay process (QuickBooks 2026).

- **Only 8% of finance teams** are fully automated in 2026; 68% still manually key invoices into ERP (Rillion / Parseur, cited by Gennai 2026).

- **14,000 UK businesses close each year** because of late payments -- 38 per day (UK Small Business Commissioner research, 2025). Cost to UK economy: ~£11 billion/year.

- **Unpaid invoices contribute to 25% of business bankruptcies** (Allianz Trade).

## The Landscape

### The Problem Has Gotten Worse, Not Better

Late payments jumped 12 percentage points in a single year -- from 47% of businesses in 2025 to 59% in 2026. Several structural factors drove this: the US experienced a record run of government shutdowns in 2025-2026 (a 43-day shutdown Oct-Nov 2025, followed by a 76-day shutdown Feb-Apr 2026), which delayed payment to an estimated 65,500 small-business federal contractors and cascaded down to subcontractors. Atradius data confirms that roughly half of all B2B invoice value is overdue, with customer cash-flow pressure as the primary driver.

The average small business doesn't have a cash buffer to absorb this. 39% can't cover one month of operating expenses. 88% experienced cash flow disruptions in the past year. And the people who are late paying are often in the same position: 24% of businesses with overdue invoices say delayed revenue was the specific reason they couldn't pay their own suppliers on time. It's a cascade.

### The Cost Is Higher Than Most Owners Realize

The obvious cost is the $17,700 average balance sitting unpaid. But the hidden costs add up fast. Owners spend 14 hours/week on collections. They pay extra fees (credit card interest, overdrafts, factoring) to access money they already earned -- 59% of them did this in the past year. After a missed payroll, employment doesn't return to pre-miss level for at least two years. And the longer an invoice goes unpaid, the recovery rate collapses: invoices 90+ days overdue have a sharply lower chance of ever being collected.

### Automation Is the Clear Differentiator

The data is unambiguous: businesses that adopt digital tools get paid faster. The QuickBooks report found a direct correlation: businesses with few late invoices are far more likely to use accounting software (56% vs 36%), cloud services (43% vs 30%), AI (20% vs 16%), and e-commerce tools (20% vs 15%) than businesses with many late invoices.

Yet adoption remains low: only 8% of finance teams are fully automated. 68% still manually key invoices. 66% still use Excel for AP tracking. The gap between knowing and doing is where Dunn lives.

### The Market Is Large and Growing

The global billing and invoicing software market was valued at ~$6.57 billion in 2026 and is projected to reach $20.04 billion by 2035 (CAGR 15.23%). The small business accounting software market alone is $7.7 billion. More than 60% of B2B invoices are now delivered electronically, but digital delivery hasn't meant digital processing -- that's the opening.

## Perspectives

### The Bull Case for Dunn

The macro conditions favor a product like Dunn. Late payments are at an all-time high and getting worse. Small businesses are actively looking for solutions -- the market is growing 15%+ annually. The pain is visceral: owners spend 14 hours/week on collections, 59% paid extra fees to access their own money, and 39% can't cover payroll after one late payment. A product that automates reminders, late fees, and recurring invoices directly addresses the #1 financial risk small businesses face. The correlation between automation adoption and getting paid faster is one of the cleanest signals in the data. Dunn isn't a nice-to-have; it's directly solving a structural problem that costs businesses $39K/year.

### The Bear Case

The competitive moat is shallow. QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks, and Wave all offer invoicing and reminders as features inside broader accounting suites. A standalone invoicing/reminder tool needs to be dramatically simpler or better than what's already integrated into the tools business owners already use. The counterargument: 74% of small businesses aren't running fully automated processes, which means the existing tools aren't solving the problem for most owners. Simplicity and focus (just reminders, fees, and recurring) could win where suites are too complex. But pricing pressure from incumbents is real -- QuickBooks can afford to give invoicing away because they make money on accounting and payroll.

## Open Questions

- How much of the 2026 late payment surge is driven by one-time macro events (federal shutdowns) vs structural trends? The QuickBooks data doesn't separate these. If the surge is partly temporary, the addressable market may shrink slightly in 2027.
- What's the specific ROI of automated reminders vs late-fee enforcement? Dunn does both, but it's unclear which feature drives the most value for customers.
- What pricing model works for a standalone invoicing tool given that incumbents bundle it free with accounting software? This is the critical go-to-market question.
- How effective are late fees in actually changing client payment behavior vs just generating revenue? The literature is thin on this.
