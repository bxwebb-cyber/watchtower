## Why Dunn Fits

NSF SBIR funds **high-risk R&D** that turns fundamental science/engineering into commercial products. Dunn's existing product is a working SaaS (rule-based reminders, late fees, recurring invoices). The R&D that needs funding is the **late-payment prediction engine** — an ML system that makes accurate payment predictions from sparse client data.

NSF's own criteria from the Project Pitch self-assessment:

| Criterion | Dunn's Fit |
|-----------|-----------|
| Breakthrough Technical Innovation | Transfer learning for sparse-data payment prediction — fundamentally different from existing invoicing tools that are purely rule-based |
| Risky, Unproven R&D | The core ML research (few-shot prediction from <5 invoices per client) is unproven — no existing invoicing product does this |
| Big Impact on Nation/Economy | 59% of US small businesses carry overdue invoices ($17.7K avg), contributing to 25% of business bankruptcies |
| Technological Defensibility | Proprietary model trained on Dunn's multi-tenant invoice data — competitors (QuickBooks, Wave) have the data but aren't doing this |
| Commercial Potential | $6.57B market growing 15% CAGR, direct ROI for users (avg $39K/year lost to late payments) |
| Qualified Team | Shipping product, working beta, founder with CS degree + applied ML capabilities |

## Honest Gap Framing

**What exists now:** Dunn is a shipped, working SaaS. Invoice creator, pre-due reminders, late-fee engine with owner approval gate, recurring invoice templates, inbound email replies, Stripe Connect, dashboard with KPI cards, 48 passing tests. It is rule-based.

**What NSF will fund:** The ML prediction pipeline — a transfer-learning model that predicts late payment risk per invoice, trained on invoice history across thousands of businesses, that improves cash flow forecasting for small business owners. This is new R&D, not a feature build.
